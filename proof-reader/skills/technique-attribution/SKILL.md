# Attribute a proof technique

Use the established name at the level actually demonstrated by the proof. A recognizable general name with a precise explanation is preferable to inventing a label for a local lemma. Treat elementary, conventionally unattributed mechanisms such as double counting as folklore when the supplied proof uses their ordinary form. Set `folklore` true. The heading stays untagged. Inside the entry, an optional `uses` source may point to an especially informative, verified application; an empty source list is entirely valid. A folklore entry never carries an `introduces` source. A particular refinement with a documented source can have its own attribution when the proof actually needs that refinement.

For an attributed technique, inspect an earlier primary paper's actual proof, construction, or explicit introduction statement. Connect the proof mechanism to an identifiable section, theorem, or page at a public HTTPS source. Link only a `paperId` that exists in the result's papers and covers a step where this technique is used. Mark `uses` when the inspected paper applies the same mechanism. Reserve `introduces` for a primary passage explicitly documenting the original contribution at the specificity named. An early publication date, author overlap, a reference label, or use of a stronger algorithm does not establish priority. For `introduces`, supply the short supporting primary-source wording (at most 20 quoted words) in `introEvidence` and explain the specific attribution at the cited locator in the paper card. For `uses`, give an empty `introEvidence`. If no defensible source is available for a specialized method, omit its attributed technique entry. Keep substantive proof details in the step checkpoints. Unverified references may not become source tags.

Give each returned literature card a compact citation key with verified authors and publication year. For several authors use initials of the family names in author order, followed by the last two digits of the year, e.g. `ALT21`. For one author a short family-name prefix is suitable, e.g. `Tar75`. Longer author lists can use a short sequence of initials and `+`. Return bare keys in the dedicated field; the reader supplies display brackets. Distinct publications with the same base key receive lowercase suffixes. Multiple locators within one publication retain the same citation key, even when their comparisons need different card IDs.
